What changes, and when

The first two waves are API-layer changes only. No new datastore, no customer-side change, and no pricing decision.

Wave 0 · weeks

Shared cache and meteringMove from per-node memory to Redis, measure hit rate and query cost, alert on the limiter, fix the KB.

Wave 1 · weeks

10-second dwell floorChange-version keys, single-flight at invalidation, and a global bulkhead on the replica as a safety net.

Wave 2 · measure

See what is leftReport residual replica load, then decide what follows from data rather than in advance.

Wave 3 · conditional

Read modelA copy of alerts and tickets outside SQL, refreshed on changes only. Only if load remains.

How the numbers are derived. Requests and queries are arithmetic: a full pull of alerts and tickets, 5 pages each, at the selected interval. Under the floor, every cache key refreshes at most 6 times per minute, which leaves page count as the only remaining multiplier. CPU demand is illustrative, at 50ms CPU per heavy query on 8 vCPU. The calibration was chosen so the default lands on the 96% measured during the June 24 incident. Real values can be extracted from a replay over one week of logs, without touching production.
